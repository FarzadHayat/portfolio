The Rational Time System (RTS) is my redesign of how we keep time. It replaces the Gregorian calendar and the 24-hour clock with rules that follow simple math. I designed the system and built a live dashboard to show it.

The Gregorian calendar has problems. Months have 28, 29, 30 or 31 days. The 1st of the month falls on a different weekday each month, so you have to check a calendar to plan anything. Quarters are uneven, which makes business and financial cycles harder. And daily time still uses base-12 and base-60 math.

RTS fixes the calendar first. A year has 13 months, and every month has 28 days, which is exactly four weeks. The 1st is always a Monday and the 28th is always a Sunday. If you know the date, you know the weekday.

Thirteen months of 28 days gives 364 days. The 365th day, and the 366th in a leap year, are Global Holidays. They sit outside every month and every week, as days of rest for the whole planet. I call them Year Day and Leap Day.

RTS then fixes the clock. A day has 10 hours, an hour has 100 minutes, and a minute has 100 seconds. That is 100,000 seconds per day. Time math becomes base-10 math, the same as the metric system.

The dashboard makes the idea concrete:

- **Converter:** a live clock that reads your standard UTC time and shows it as decimal time and an RTS date.
- **Calendar grid:** every month has the same shape, so the layout never moves. Normal digital calendars shift every month.
- **Holiday panels:** Year Day and Leap Day get their own panels, to show that they sit outside the normal flow of time.
- **Daylight globe:** a 3D globe shows the real-time line between day and night across the Earth. It is a reminder that RTS is one time for the whole planet.

The look takes ideas from sci-fi and aerospace dashboards: deep space gradients, dark frosted glass panels and monospaced fonts. I wanted the system to feel official and precise.
